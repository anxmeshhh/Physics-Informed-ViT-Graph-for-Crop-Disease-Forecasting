1 Overview

The problem

  • Existing systems classify a leaf after symptoms appear
  • They ignore weather, which is what drives infection
  • They treat every field as isolated

Our approach

  • Leaf image → what disease is present
  • Real weather → will infection spread
  • Neighbouring farms → is it arriving
  • Forecast 1, 3, 5, 7 days ahead

Why climate matters

  • Late blight → cool + wet (17°C)
  • Powdery mildew → humid air, rain kills it
  • Spider mites → hot + dry
  • Opposite responses; pixels cannot see this
Evaluation integrity. PlantVillage photographs each leaf 2.65× on average, so a random split leaks 35,246 of 54,305 images (65%). All results use leaf-disjoint splits — zero shared leaves.

Shading is a 1° grid (about 110 km) of the weather recorded across the region, pinned to each district's own reading, and drawn on land only. Circles are the monitored districts. Risk is computed from the weather recorded at each district. For a chosen location it combines the weather recorded at that exact spot with disease arriving from nearby districts on the wind, as simulated by the project's epidemic model. The 7-day outlook replays the weather that actually followed (a hindcast); in deployment the same calculation runs on the weather forecast. Recommended actions are general guidance — product and dose follow the state agriculture department.
